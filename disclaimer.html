Available independent research consistently shows that most day traders lose money:

Barber, Lee, Liu, & Odean (2014). Do Day Traders Rationally Learn About Their Ability? — Studied 3.7 billion trades on the Taiwan Stock Exchange (1992–2006). Found only 9.81% of day-trading volume was generated by predictably profitable traders, and these traders constituted less than 3% of all day traders on an average day.
Garvey & Murphy (2005). The Profitability of Active Stock Traders, Journal of Applied Finance. — Studied 1,146 brokerage accounts day trading U.S. markets (March–June 2000). Only 50% were profitable, with an average net profit of $16,619.
Jordan & Diltz (2003). The Profitability of Day Traders, Financial Analysts Journal. — Studied 334 brokerage accounts day trading U.S. markets (February 1998 – October 1999). Only 35% were profitable; only 14% generated profits in excess of $10,000.

9. Penny Stocks and Low-Priced Securities

Trading penny stocks and other low-priced securities involves additional risks beyond those associated with larger-cap securities, including:

  • Limited publicly available information about the company's management, operations, and financial condition.
  • No minimum listing standards — many penny stocks trade on the OTC market without the reporting requirements of major exchanges.
  • Liquidity risk — these securities may be difficult to buy or sell quickly, affecting the price you receive.
  • High volatility — large price swings can occur in very short timeframes.
  • Fraud risk — low-priced, thinly traded securities are more susceptible to price manipulation and fraud.

If you choose to trade penny stocks, you do so understanding and accepting these elevated risks.
